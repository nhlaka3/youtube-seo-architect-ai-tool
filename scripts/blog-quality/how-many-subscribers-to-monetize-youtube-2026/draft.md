# How Many Subscribers Do You Need to Monetize on YouTube in 2026? (Exact Numbers)

The short answer: **you need 1,000 subscribers** to start earning revenue sharing from YouTube. But subscribers alone are not enough. Full monetization requires **1,000 subscribers AND either 4,000 public watch hours in the last 12 months OR 10 million valid Shorts views in the last 90 days**. Hit one of those pairings and you get AdSense revenue, channel memberships, Super Thanks — the full partner program.

There is a second, lower gate most creators miss. **At 500 subscribers** you can already apply for the **fan-funding tier** (memberships, Super Thanks, the affiliate and paid-products program) with 3,000 watch hours or 3 million Shorts views. I'll walk through both gates, the exact view definitions that trip people up, and the order that gets you to 1,000 fastest.

## The Two Monetization Thresholds: 500 vs 1,000 Subscribers

YouTube runs two entry gates. Both are gated by subscribers, so the number is the fixed part.

| Threshold | Watch hours (12 mo) | OR Shorts views (90 days) | What you get |
|---|---|---|---|
| **500 subscribers** | 3,000 | 3 million | Fan-funding tier: memberships, Super Thanks, affiliate (no AdSense ad-partner revenue) |
| **1,000 subscribers** | 4,000 | 10 million | Full YPP: AdSense ad revenue, revenue share, more monetization tools |

The useful pattern: the **subscriber number is fixed**, but the *watch requirement gives you a choice* — long-form hours or Shorts views. A long-form channel hits 1,000 subs and 4,000 watch hours and qualifies. A Shorts-first channel hits 1,000 subs and 10 million views and qualifies. You do not need both the hours and the views; they are alternatives.

These numbers are the current public thresholds on YouTube's own monetization policy page (support.google.com/youtube, retrieved 2026-08-07). YouTube has revised the entry levels since 2023, so verify against the live policy before you set expectations.

<div class="tip-card"><strong>💡 Key fact:</strong> The 1,000-subscriber number applies to the *channel*, not to individual videos. Every video on a monetized channel can earn once the channel bar is met and your videos follow the monetization policy.</div>

## What the "1,000 Subscribers" Really Buys

Hitting 1,000 subscribers is the milestone creators recognise. It's the point your channel passes from a hobby into an income-eligible channel in YouTube's system. It is not a switch — it is an **invitation to apply**. Reaching the number makes you eligible to apply for the YouTube Partner Program; you still have to apply, pass review, and enable ads.

The practical value of the bar is twofold:

- **Ad revenue**: Once eligible and accepted, you can monetize long-form videos with AdSense ads. You earn a share of the ad revenue.
- **Trust signal**: someone at 1,000 subscribers has real organic proof that a channel can hold an audience. Sponsors and affiliates use it as a benchmark even before ad revenue matters.

The 1,000 number is not the only thing YouTube weighs. Your channel also must have no active YouTube Community Guidelines strikes, have a linked AdSense account, live in a supported country, and follow the partner program policies.

<div class="warning-callout"><strong>⚠️ WARNING:</strong> A subscriber count above 1,000 with no watch hours gets you nothing. Several creators hit the number through giveaways only to discover the watch-hour gate still blocks AdSense. Optimize both gates in parallel.</div>

## What the 500-Subscriber Fan-Funding Tier Adds

In 2022 YouTube lowered the entry point with a new tier. At **500 subscribers** you become eligible to apply for the partner program's **fan-funding tier** even before full ad monetization. That gives you:

- YouTube channel memberships
- Super Thanks (tips on videos)
- The affiliate and shopping program

The trade-off is clear: at 500 subscribers you can earn from your audience directly (memberships, tips, commissions) but you do **not** get broad ad revenue — that comes only at 1,000 plus the watch requirement. If you have a small but devoted audience, the 500 tier is a real way to start earning while you work toward 1,000.

This is a genuinely useful intermediate step. It means the answer to "how many subscribers to monetize" is **not one number** — it is 500 for direct support revenue and 1,000 for full ad-based monetization.

## The Bare Minimum Requirements (Checklist, Verified)

Make sure the full list is true before you apply, per YouTube's partner policy terms (support.google.com/youtube, retrieved 2026-08-07):

1. **Subscribers:** 1,000 for full ad monetization (500 for the fan-funding tier).
2. **Watch requirement (one of two):** 4,000 valid public watch hours in the last 12 months, OR 10 million valid public Shorts views in the last 90 days.
3. **AdSense account:** A linked, approved, distinct Google AdSense account.
4. **Policy compliance:** No active YouTube Community Guidelines strikes, and content that follows the channel monetization policies.
5. **Country & age:** Your channel must be in a country or region where the YPP operates, and the account must meet YouTube's age requirement.

Watch hours count only when they come from **public videos that do not violate policy**. Time from for-kids-locked content, repeated uploads, or private viewing is frequently excluded. That exclusion is the most common reason an otherwise-qualified channel sees its "hours" drop at the point of application.

## How to Hit 1,000 Subscribers + the Watch Gate (6 Steps)

The subscriber number is the blocker for most long-form channels, so treat it as a number you can drive — not something to wait for.

### 1. Make the number a measurable target using a growth calculator
Enter your current subscriber count and average new-subs-per-week into the [subscriber growth calculator](/tools/subscriber-growth-calculator). It turns a vague "hoping" into a weekly target and a date. If you need 400 subs at 10 a week, that is about 10 months — and you can accelerate it.

### 2. Convert every view into a subscribe action
"Like and subscribe" is overused but works when you give a reason. Pair it with a specific value promise: "Subscribe so the next guide in this series hits your feed." Put the subscribe ask near the end of the video rather than the opening, and conversions follow.

### 3. Niche down, and use long-form to satisfy the watch-hour gate
Long-form is your headroom for watch hours. A single well-made piece satisfies a large share of the 4,000-hour gate quickly. Research a "how to" question with clear search intent you can fully answer in 8–12 minutes; the algorithm rewards the answer-first structure (the same reason this article answers directly).

### 4. Use Shorts as a complementary 10-million-view path
If your niche works better vertical, run Shorts. The alternative track — 10M views in 90 days — is more forgiving for newer channels with momentum spikes. Many creators reach the 1,000-subscriber gate through Shorts and the view gate through occasional longer uploads.

### 5. Apply the moment you qualify
Do not wait until you're "sure." The moment you hit both gates, apply in YouTube Studio. Review typically takes a few days, and the revenue clock starts after acceptance. Waiting just costs you time.

### 6. Keep the channel inside policy
Avoid reused content with only superficial changes and misleading thumbnails or titles. A monetization review that catches a policy issue resets the clock. Submit a clean channel and acceptance is usually smooth.

## Common Subscriber-Monetization Myths (Fixed)

- **"1,000 subscribers pays you money."** No — it only entitles you to apply and earn from ads once the watch requirement passes. You are not paid for reaching the count.
- **"Shorts can't build toward the 1,000."** Wrong. Shorts-subscriber counts stack exactly like long-form, and the 10M Shorts-view track counts as a direct qualifying path.
- **"Buying subscribers moves the gate."** Bought or forged subscribers violate policy, hurt momentum, and the watch-hour gate still blocks you. It is wasted effort and it can damage the channel.
- **"Once monetized, always monetized."** Monetization is conditional on continued policy compliance. A strike or a review that finds reused content can pause the income.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many subscribers do I need for YouTube to pay me?
The full ad-share bar is **1,000 subscribers** plus 4,000 watch hours or 10M Shorts views. For the fan-funding tier (memberships, Super Thanks, affiliate) you can apply at **500 subscribers** with 3,000 hours or 3M Shorts views. Subscribers alone do not pay anyone.

### Can I make money at 500 subscribers?
Yes — but only from direct-fan revenue: channel memberships, Super Thanks and tips, and the affiliate and shopping program. Standard AdSense ad-revenue sharing only applies once you cross 1,000 plus the watch requirement.

### What counts as a "valid" watch hour?
Public viewing of long-form videos that do not violate monetization policies. Time from content marked as made for kids, repeated re-uploads, or spam is excluded, and private or unlisted views do not count either.

### Do Shorts subscribers count toward 1,000?
Yes. Shorts subscribers stack toward the 1,000 gate exactly like long-form subscribers. What differs is the watch side — 4,000 long-form hours OR 10M Shorts views in 90 days.

### What happens if I monetize then my channel gets a strike?
A Community Guidelines strike can pause monetization temporarily, and a policy-terms breach can reset the program. You can re-apply if eligibility is regained or the strike expires. Eligibility is not permanently owned.

## Conclusion

The exact answer: **500 subscribers** opens fan-funding; **1,000 subscribers** gives you full YouTube monetization. The subscriber count only works together with the watch gate — 4,000 long-form hours or 10M Shorts views in the window. Plan both gates, treat the number as a target you drive, and apply the moment you qualify.
