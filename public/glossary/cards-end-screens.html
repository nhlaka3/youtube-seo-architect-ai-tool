YouTube Features

Cards and End Screens

Interactive elements on YouTube videos that link viewers to other videos, playlists, channels, or external sites during and after playback.

📖 All Terms 📊 Analytics 🤖 Algorithm 🔍 SEO 💰 Monetization 📝 Content Strategy ⚙️ Features

What Is Cards and End Screens?

Cards and end screens are YouTube's native promotional tools that keep viewers on your channel. Cards appear as small icons during video playback (top-right corner) and can link to other videos, playlists, channels, or poll viewers. End screens appear in the last 20 seconds of a video with up to 4 clickable elements. To maximize their effectiveness: add a card linking to a related video within the first 30 seconds, use end screens to suggest 2-3 videos or a playlist, promote your best content (not the video they just watched), and include a subscribe button on end screens. Both features increase watch time and session time — two of the algorithm's most important signals.

Why Cards and End Screens Matters for YouTube SEO

Cards and End Screens is a powerful YouTube feature that can significantly enhance your channel's reach, engagement, or revenue when used correctly. Many creators overlook or underutilize YouTube's built-in features, missing out on free tools that can boost performance without additional production effort. Channels that actively leverage cards and end screens see higher engagement rates, better retention, and stronger algorithm performance because they're using the platform's native advantages. Mastering this feature gives you a competitive edge over creators who focus only on content quality while ignoring the distribution and engagement tools YouTube provides.

How to Optimize for Cards and End Screens

To make the most of cards and end screens, start by exploring the feature in YouTube Studio to understand all its capabilities — many features have hidden options most creators never use. Watch YouTube's official tutorials on this feature and study how top creators in your niche utilize it. Experiment with one aspect of the feature per video, tracking which approach gets the best results. Document what works and what doesn't so you can build a repeatable system. Share your learnings with your community — engagement with your experiments often provides valuable feedback and ideas for improvement.
