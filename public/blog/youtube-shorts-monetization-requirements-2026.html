What "Monetize Shorts" Actually Means in 2026

"Monetize Shorts" means your Shorts earn a share of ad revenue from the Shorts Feed, exactly the way long-form earns a share of ad revenue. YouTube folded Shorts into the Partner Program in Feb 2023, replacing the older Shorts Fund, so eligible creators are paid through the same ad-revenue system rather than a fixed bonus pool.

Three terms you'll see everywhere, defined plainly:

  • YouTube Partner Program (YPP) is YouTube's monetization program. Membership unlocks ad revenue, memberships, Super Thanks, and shopping on your channel.
  • AdSense for YouTube is the payout account Google attaches to your channel. You need one active account at review time or you can't get paid.
  • RPM (revenue per mille) is your earnings per 1,000 views. Shorts RPM is typically far lower than long-form RPM because Shorts ad inventory is cheaper (see our RPM explainer).

There is no separate "Shorts-only program." The requirements you searched for are the YPP thresholds — once you meet them and are approved, your eligible Shorts start earning. If you're new to how the Shorts feed ranks content, start with our Shorts algorithm glossary entry.

The Exact Thresholds (from YouTube's official help page)

YouTube Help, "YouTube Partner Program overview & eligibility," lists two eligibility options verbatim (answer/72851, retrieved Aug 6, 2026):

Route A — Long-form: 1,000 subscribers with 4,000 valid public watch hours in the last 12 months.

Route B — Shorts: 1,000 subscribers with 10 million valid public Shorts views in the last 90 days (eligibility terms).

Meet either one, plus these conditions:

  • 2-Step Verification turned on for the Google/YouTube account.
  • Live in a YouTube Partner Program-eligible country.
  • No active Community Guidelines strikes.
  • Advanced features access enabled on the channel.
  • One active AdSense-for-YouTube account to receive payments.

There is also an earlier-access tier (fan-funding features only — Super Thanks, memberships, shopping — not ad revenue): 500 subscribers + 3 public uploads in 90 days + 3,000 long-form watch hours OR 3 million Shorts views in 90 days (answer/72857, retrieved Aug 6, 2026). It unlocks tips and memberships but does not get you Shorts ad revenue.

Two Roads: Shorts Views vs Watch Hours (and the catch)

The most common misconception: "my Shorts get millions of views, so I'll clear the 4,000-hour bar." You will not — because Shorts watch hours do not count toward the 4,000-hour threshold. YouTube is explicit: any public watch hours from Shorts in the Shorts Feed won't count toward the 4,000 public watch hours requirement.

So the routes are genuinely separate:

Route Subscribers The big number Time window Format
A (long-form) 1,000 4,000 valid watch hours last 12 months long-form only
B (Shorts) 1,000 10M valid Shorts views last 90 days Shorts Feed only

Shorts-only channels realistically chase Route B. The catch is momentum: both counters are rolling windows, so a single viral spike won't carry you — you need sustained output.

What Counts as a "Valid Public Shorts View"

Google defines "what the count as a valid public Shorts view" precisely, because it decides whether your 10M counts:

  • The Short must be public — private, unlisted, and deleted Shorts don't count.
  • It must be an engaged view that appears in the Shorts Feed.
  • It is NOT a view from ad campaigns, private/unlisted/deleted videos, or image posts appearing in the Shorts Feed.
  • It must be original content — reuploads or heavy re-edits of others' content can be invalidated under the reuse rules.

The Timeline: How Shorts Started Earning Money

  • May 2021: YouTube launched the Shorts Fund, "a $100M fund distributed over the course of 2021–2022" (Introducing the YouTube Shorts Fund, blog.youtube, May 11, 2021).
  • Feb 1, 2023: Shorts entered the Partner Program and ad-revenue sharing turned on — the switch from a bonus pool to real per-creator ad revenue.
  • Mar 28, 2024: YouTube's update ("One year in, revenue sharing on Shorts") reported over 70 billion average daily Shorts views since launch, that more than 25% of channels in the Partner Program now earn from the Shorts revenue stream, and that more than 80% of creators who joined YPP via the Shorts eligibility thresholds also earn through other YPP features (blog.youtube, Mar 28, 2024).

That last stat is the one to quote when someone says Shorts aren't real revenue: the majority of Shorts-joined creators quickly branch into other monetization features, which is the durable part of Shorts monetization.

How Much Shorts Actually Pay (the honest answer)

Google publishes no official "Shorts RPM" figure. YouTube's position: earnings vary by region, format, and content. So any "$0.05–$0.40 per 1,000 Shorts views" you see in a blog post is a third-party estimate, not an official number.

What IS official and quotable:

  • Eligible creators earn a share of the Shorts Feed ad-revenue pool — the same revenue system as long-form, since Feb 2023. It works like this: ads run in the Shorts Feed, YouTube pools that revenue, and eligible creators get a share (how ad revenue works).
  • A Short only earns ad revenue if it uses original audio or audio from YouTube's licensed library — Shorts using unlicensed music generally don't monetize.
  • Industry observers report Shorts RPM runs well below long-form RPM for the same creator.

The reliable way to know your real number: once monetized, read YouTube Studio → Analytics → Revenue. Your own Studio number beats anyone's estimate.

Rules That Trip People Up (and can void your 10M)

These official quirks cost creators their shot:

  1. Shorts watch hours don't count toward the 4,000-hour route — only long-form hours do.
  2. Sound is everything. Shorts with unlicensed music earn no ad revenue; use original audio or YouTube's licensed library.
  3. No reuploads. Re-edits of others' content don't qualify as valid original views under YPP reuse rules — and they invite strikes.
  4. The 90-day clock. The 10M and 4,000 figures only look back to the window. Aged-out views stop counting, so consistency beats one-time spikes.

A Realistic 6-Month Shorts Monetization Plan

  1. Turn on 2-Step Verification today. It's a hard requirement nobody remembers and it blocks advanced features.
  2. Pick your route. Shorts-only? Your bar is 10M valid Shorts Feed views in 90 days at 1,000 subs. A 1–2 Shorts/day cadence is the common pace among creators who clear it.
  3. Fix your audio + format. Use original audio or the licensed library; never clip copyrighted audio.
  4. Win the first two seconds. "Engaged views" rewards Shorts that hold the viewer. On Shorts, the first frame is your thumbnail — audit your hooks with the free CTR & impressions calculator and check your tag strategy with the YouTube tag generator.
  5. Keep winners public + original. No private drafts of big hits; make them public to count.
  6. Apply once you're over the bar. Studio → Earn → Apply, then await review.
  7. Set up AdSense for YouTube before applying so there's a payout account attached at review time.

FAQ

Can I monetize Shorts with 1,000 subs and 10M views? Yes — that's exactly Route B's threshold (1,000 subs + 10M valid public Shorts views in 90 days). Keep views public, original, and in the Shorts Feed.

Does Shorts watch time count toward the 4,000-watch-hour requirement? No. YouTube states Shorts Feed watch hours don't count toward the 4,000-hour threshold; that route requires long-form watch hours.

Is there a payout minimum for Shorts? No separate number beyond the YPP bar. You need one active AdSense-for-YouTube account; after approval, earnings pay out on AdSense's usual schedule.

Can I earn anything before 10M views? At the earlier-access tier (500 subs) you can unlock fan-funding features (Super Thanks, memberships, shopping) — but not Shorts advertisable revenue.

Do licensed-music Shorts monetize at all? Shorts with your audio or YouTube's licensed library can earn; Shorts with unlicensed music generally earn no ad revenue.
