Deep Dive: YouTube Shorts Monetization through Ad Revenue Sharing

YouTube Shorts monetization requirements in 2026 are strict, but creators can earn money through ad revenue sharing. This option works by displaying ads before, during, or after Shorts videos. Creators earn a portion of the ad revenue based on the number of views and clicks on the ads.

Pricing varies depending on the advertiser, ad format, and target audience. On average, creators can earn between $0.01 and $0.03 per ad view. While this may not seem like a lot, popular creators with millions of views can earn significant amounts of money. For example, a creator with 1 million views per month can earn around $100 to $300 per month from ad revenue sharing.

Ease of use is relatively high, as creators don't need to do much to enable ad revenue sharing on their Shorts videos. They simply need to meet YouTube's monetization requirements, which include having at least 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours in the past 12 months. Once they've met these requirements, they can apply for the YouTube Partner Program (YPP) and start earning money from ads.

Results vary depending on the creator's audience, content, and engagement. For example, a creator who produces high-quality, engaging content that resonates with their audience may see higher ad revenue than a creator who produces low-quality content. Here are a few examples:

The Verdict: Which Should YOU Choose?

When considering YouTube Shorts monetization requirements in 2026, creators have two main options: focus on traditional YouTube videos or prioritize Shorts. The right choice depends on your content style and audience.

Choose traditional YouTube videos if you're a creator who produces high-quality, in-depth content that resonates with your audience. This format is ideal for vloggers, reviewers, and educators who can engage viewers for longer periods.

Choose YouTube Shorts if you're a creator who excels at producing short-form, attention-grabbing content. This format is perfect for comedians, musicians, and artists who can convey their message in 60 seconds or less.

⚠️ WATCH OUT: Don't try to force long-form content into Shorts or vice versa. This can lead to low engagement and poor monetization.
