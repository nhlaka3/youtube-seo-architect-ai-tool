Step 1: Harvest Autocomplete (5 Minutes)

Type your broad topic into YouTube search. Note every autocomplete suggestion. Then expand each with modifiers:

ModifierExample expansion
for beginners"drone tips" → "drone photography tips for beginners"
2026"best AI tools" → "best AI tools for Etsy sellers 2026"
without / under $X"home workout" → "home workout no equipment 30 min"
in [country]"online jobs for students" → "online jobs for students in Nigeria 2026"
step by step / explained simply"credit score" → "credit score explained simply 2026"

Each expansion is a candidate keyword. Collect 30–50 before scoring.

Step 2: Check Competitor Gaps (5 Minutes)

Search your top 5 candidates. For each, check the top 5 videos:

  • Views: Under 50K = green light. Over 500K with big channels = skip.
  • Channel size: Top channels under 50K subs = no authority yet.
  • Freshness: No "2026" in titles = outdated, you can outrank with a fresh video.
  • Gaps: Questions in comments that competitors don't answer = your next video angle.

Extract their tags (view page source → search "keywords") and note long-tail phrases that repeat across videos. Those are YouTube's topic signals. Use Tag Generator to generate your own optimized set.

Step 3: Score with AI (5 Minutes)

Run your 30–50 candidates through Keyword Discovery. It scores each on:

FactorWhat it meansTarget
VolumeHow many search itReal demand (not zero)
CompetitionHow strong incumbents areLow / Medium
IntentInformational / Commercial / TransactionalMatch your video format
OpportunityVolume vs competition balance80+ = film it
💡 Intent match matters: "Best X" needs a list/comparison. "How to X" needs a tutorial. "X review" needs a review. Mismatched intent = no rank, even for low competition keywords.

Step 4: Build a Cluster (Not a Single Video)

Pick your top 1 keyword. Plan 3–5 videos:

  1. Main how-to / list video (primary keyword in title + first description sentence)
  2. Beginner version (same topic + "for beginners")
  3. Mistakes / comparison version
  4. Location or year variant

Clusters build topical authority. See best keywords by niche for cluster examples.

Common Mistakes

  • Chasing broad head terms as a small channel.
  • Copying competitor tags verbatim without filtering.
  • Publishing one video instead of a cluster.
  • Skipping the year qualifier.
  • Ignoring description + tags — see Tags 2026 guide.

How do I find low competition keywords?

Autocomplete + modifiers, competitor gap analysis, AI scoring. Look for under-50K-view incumbents.

What makes a keyword low competition?

Real demand but weak incumbents — low views, small channels, outdated metadata.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to find a low competition keyword?

With the method in this guide, 15-30 minutes per video idea. Autocomplete mining takes about 5 minutes, competitor gap analysis 10 minutes, and scoring 5 minutes with a tool.

Do I need a paid tool to find low competition keywords?

No. YouTube autocomplete plus manual SERP checks cover 90% of the work. Paid tools mainly save time on bulk scoring and volume estimates.

What view count means low competition on YouTube?

A practical threshold: if the first page of results mostly shows videos under 100K views, the keyword is low competition. Under 50K for all results is very low competition.

How do I check YouTube keyword difficulty for free?

Search the keyword on YouTube in incognito mode and look at three things: view counts of the top results, subscriber counts of the channels, and how recent the results are. High views, big channels, and fresh uploads mean high difficulty.

What should I do after I find a low competition keyword?

Match your title to the exact keyword, use it in the first 100 characters of your description, generate tags with our free Tag Generator, then cluster 3-5 related keywords into a content series so you build topical authority fast.
