  • Mix 5 types: evergreen how-to, long-tail niche, question-based, trending AI, comparison — see tables by niche below.

Not all keywords are equal. A broad phrase like "fitness tips" might get millions of searches — and millions of competitors. A specific phrase like "30 min home workout no equipment" gets fewer searches but far less competition, so a small channel can rank for it in weeks. In 2026, the winners mix keyword types strategically instead of chasing one.

Strategy by Channel Size

  • New (0–1K subs): Long-tail only. Target phrases where top videos have under 50K views. Add "2026" + location.
  • Growing (1K–50K): Mix long-tail + question-based. Start testing comparison keywords.
  • Established (50K+): Add evergreen how-to + trending AI topics for spikes. Defend with clusters.

Common Mistakes

  • Targeting broad head terms as a small channel.
  • Publishing isolated videos instead of clusters.
  • Ignoring the year qualifier ("2026" cuts competition).
  • Skipping intent match — "best X" needs a list/comparison, not a vlog.
  • Forgetting tags + description — see YouTube Tags 2026.

Frequently asked questions

What are the best keywords for a new YouTube channel?

Long-tail how-to and question-based keywords with specific qualifiers: 'how to [task] on [device]', '[task] for beginners 2026', '[tool] vs [tool] for [use case]'. They have lower competition and higher intent than broad head terms.

Which niche has the lowest competition on YouTube?

Highly specific B2B and hobby niches (niche software tutorials, specialized crafts, local trades) have the lowest competition. Broad entertainment niches like gaming and vlogs are the most saturated.

Are niche keywords better than trending keywords?

Niche keywords compound — they build topical authority and keep earning views for years. Trending keywords spike then die. Use trending only when you can publish within 48 hours of the trend.

How do I know if a keyword is too competitive?

Check the first page of results. If every video has over 500K views, is from a channel with 1M+ subscribers, and was published recently, it is too competitive for a new channel. Find the same topic with a narrower qualifier instead.

Should I target the same keywords as big channels?

Only through narrower variations. Instead of 'photoshop tutorial', target 'photoshop tutorial for product photography beginners'. Same audience intent, far lower competition.
