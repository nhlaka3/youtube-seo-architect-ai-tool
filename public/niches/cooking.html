📋 Cooking & Food Channels: SEO Strategies That Work

Put the Dish Name in Your Title First

Start your title with the recipe or dish name — "Creamy Garlic Pasta in 15 Minutes" outperforms "How to Make Quick Pasta." Viewers search for dish names, not cooking techniques. Front-load the food name within the first 40 characters.

Use Recipe Keywords in Description

Include all ingredients, cooking times, and dietary tags (vegan, gluten-free, keto) in your description. This helps YouTube match your video to recipe searches. Structure your description with clear timestamps for each cooking step.

Close-Up Thumbnails Drive Clicks

Cooking thumbnails with close-up shots of the finished dish perform 40% better than wide shots. Use warm, appetizing colors (reds, oranges, golds). Add text overlays with the dish name in a bold, readable font.

Optimize for How-To and Recipe Searches

Cooking videos rank for two types of searches: specific recipes ("chicken tikka masala recipe") and technique queries ("how to knead dough"). Create separate videos optimized for each type rather than combining them.

Use Dietary Tags for Discovery

Include dietary labels in your title, description, and tags: "vegan," "gluten-free," "keto," "low-carb," "high-protein." These are highly searched modifiers that can dramatically expand your reach.

Film in Square or Vertical Format for Shorts

Cooking content performs exceptionally well as YouTube Shorts. Film vertical clips of key cooking moments — sizzling pans, pouring sauces, final plating — and post them as Shorts to drive channel discovery.
